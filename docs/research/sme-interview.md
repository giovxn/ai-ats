# SME interview: OVI (Tim and/or Chris playing a recruiter)

Thursday 26 Nov 2026 · 1:30–1:45pm Gulf Standard Time · 15 min

**Goal:** learn how a recruiter really screens and where it hurts, then pick the Block 4 twist from that. The Decision Mirror is one candidate idea, not the final choice. If they describe a bigger pain, follow it.

**Rule:** they talk ~80% of the time. Don't pitch until minute 10. Ask about the past ("last time"), not opinions ("would you…").

## Plan

| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Set-up |
| 1–10 | Their process and pain |
| 10–13 | Test the idea |
| 13–15 | Close |

### 0–1 · Set-up
> "Thanks for doing this. I'd like to understand how you screen candidates and what's painful about it. At the end I'll run one idea past you. Can I take notes?"

Then: "What kind of company and roles are you hiring for?" (Sets the scene for the role-play.)

### 1–10 · Process and pain (pick 5–6; the ★ ones matter most)
1. "Walk me through the last role you hired for, from the moment CVs came in."
2. "Roughly how many applicants, and how many did you actually read?"
3. "What makes you reject someone in the first 30 seconds?"
4. ★ "How do you keep the same bar for the 5th CV and the 150th?"
5. ★ "Has a hiring manager ever rejected people for something that wasn't in the job description? What happened?"
6. ★ "When someone asks why a candidate was rejected, how do you answer? Where is that written down?"
7. "Who else is involved in the decision, and where do you disagree?"
8. "What's the most annoying part of your ATS or process today?"
9. "If you could fix one thing in screening tomorrow, what would it be?"

**Dig deeper** every time something sounds painful: "Tell me about the last time." · "Why?" · "What did you do about it?" · "How often?"

### 10–13 · Test the idea (say it in two sentences, then stop talking)
> "One idea: when you reject someone and give a reason, the ATS checks whether someone you moved forward shows *less* evidence for that same reason, and asks if that's intentional. It also notices when a reason keeps coming up that isn't in the job description, and asks whether to add it or stop using it."

Then:
- "Would that help you or annoy you? Why?"
- "When would you turn it off?"
- "What would make you trust or ignore an alert like that?"
- "Would you want it to block you, or just ask?"

If they're lukewarm, don't defend it. Ask: "What would make it worth using?"

If there's time, float a second idea in one sentence and compare:
> "Or: every requirement in the job ad gets checked against each CV with a quote as evidence, so you can see *why* someone fits without a score."

Then: "If you could only have one of those, which one, and why?"

If they raised a bigger pain earlier, skip the pitches and dig into that instead. That pain might be the twist.

### 13–15 · Close
- "Anything I should have asked but didn't?"
- "Is it OK if I quote you in my presentation?"
- "Thanks, that changes [one thing] in what I build."

## Show progress?
Only if they ask, or there's time left at the end: 30 seconds of the pipeline board, then back to questions. The call is for learning about them, not demoing.

## What to expect
- They'll play a recruiter or hiring manager, possibly at a small or medium company. Answers may be short or vague on purpose, to see if you dig deeper.
- They may challenge the idea ("recruiters hate being questioned"). That's useful: ask what would change their mind.
- They're also seeing how you think: open questions, listening, following up, not selling.

**Interview feedback:** asking for the *one* top pain was the strongest part (Tim). Should have gone one level deeper on the step-by-step process: where CVs are downloaded and stored (Chris).

**Verdict after the call:**
- Pain confirmed? **Partly.** Consistency and unwritten criteria were not pains (unwritten criteria are seen as legitimate), so the Decision Mirror isn't backed by this interview. Strong pains: volume (900 CVs never read), long CVs skimmed to the last 2 roles, manual tracking, rejected candidates lost for good.
- What changes in the build: pick the twist from the pains above (see the chat decision), not the Mirror.
- Best quote for the presentation: "we stopped looking at CVs after number 100."
